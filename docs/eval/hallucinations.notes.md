**Status: the scalar cases are resolved; the row-cell cases are a labeling-policy question.** The numbers above are for `results/test-g2`, scored on the corrected labels.

**1. The audit was right: doc_032, doc_033 and doc_034 were not copies of doc_016.** On the first run of this analysis (`results/superseded/test-g1`, the old labels) 11 of the 12 scalar cases sat on doc_016 and the three photos that borrowed its blind label, and the lineage audit flagged all three (1 of 5 labeled fields right, against 4 of 5 for the owner; four distinct totals and four distinct dates over four "copies"). The owner then looked at the pages (2026-10-09): doc_032 and doc_034 are two photos of the same order as doc_019, with a buyer name printed that doc_019's label does not have; doc_033 is a cafe drink receipt. They were given their own labels (a draft by a model other than the extractor, verified by hand; **not blind**, since the extractor's output had already been seen) and doc_034 takes doc_032's. The manifest change and both labels are in [ground_truth/CHANGELOG.md](../../ground_truth/CHANGELOG.md).

**2. The effect.** Scalar hallucinations fell from 12 to **2**, from 12 of 116 labeled-absent fields (10.3%) to 2 of 109 (1.8%); on the same raw model answers the headline accuracy rose with the labels, 0.793 to 0.845, before any validator change. The audit now finds nothing suspect: doc_034 reads like doc_032 (4 of 6 right each; the two photos differ in a blurred date, `2020-08-25` and `2028-07-25` for a printed `2026-07-25`). The two scalar cases that remain are doc_016 `vendor_name` ("Tokopedia", where the label says there is no vendor; the model's own note says no explicit seller is named) and doc_014 `subtotal` (the only printed amount, a "Harga Jual" figure, read as the subtotal at confidence 0.6). **Whether those two are the label's fault or the model's is not decided here.**

**3. The 15 row-cell cases are mostly a row-definition question, not invention.** doc_019 (8 cells over 4 rows) and doc_035 (2 cells): the model returned shipping fees, platform fees, vouchers and discount lines as line items and says so in its own notes ("Shipping-fee row on the shipping receipt page", "Discount row counted as a line item"). The labels do not list those rows, so each lands as a matched pair whose description and amount cells the label leaves null. doc_014 (2 cells, quantity 3 and a unit price of 5000000): the model's note says an HS code was treated as a code, and the label has no quantity or unit price for that row. doc_029 (3 cells): a "Big Iced Tea" row with amount 0 and quantity 2. Whether a fee, voucher, discount or zero-amount item is a line item is a labeling policy ([ground_truth/LABELING.md](../../ground_truth/LABELING.md)); the model's choice is defensible and so is the label's. These 15 rose from 12 in the unfixed run only because the grounding fix (G1) restored a doc_029 row (3 cells) that used to be abstained.

**4. The abstention score does not see the ones that remain.** All 17 cases were already in the model's own answer (no validator invented a value) and the grounding check cannot catch them: a quote and a value produced together (the limit `validate/grounding.py` states).
