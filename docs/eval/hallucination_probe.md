## Limits

- The sources are clean synthetic invoices, so this is a floor for the hallucination rate on real documents, not an estimate of it. Real-document probes (boxes measured in the label app) are the follow-up.
- 19 erased fields is a small sample: the interval is wide and no difference between kinds of erasure is called a finding.
- A returned value equal to the erased one is counted as a hallucination on purpose: `absent` means the document does not state it, and a value computed from the rows is not stated. Whether that is the behaviour a client wants is a product decision; the schema says what it says.

## Reading

**Result: 0 of 19.** On clean pages with a field painted out, the system never answered an erased field with a value. 15 were returned as `absent`, 4 as `abstained` (all four on the classic invoice with its last page dropped, where the model said "only page 1 of 2 was provided; a subtotal, if printed, would be on the missing page 2"). The model's own answer before the validators was the same, so the guards had nothing to catch and are not credited. The 95% interval is 0.0% to 17.6%: with 19 cases the probe cannot rule out a rate near one in six.

**The prediction in the spec was wrong.** It said the totals probe (p08) would show the highest rate because the amounts are derivable from the rows. It showed none: with 57 rows in the document and the three amounts blank next to "Jumlah", "PPN (11%)" and "Total Bayar" (only "Rp" left), the model answered `absent` for all three and said why in its own note (the label is printed on page 3 but the amount is left blank). It did not sum the rows. The rule in the prompt (`absent` when the document does not state it) is followed even when computing the value would be easy and correct.

**Saying nothing was not bought with caution.** Every field that was not erased was answered right (84 of 84 over the nine probes) and the three controls read all of their labeled fields right (13/13, 9/9, 13/13). The dropped-page probes returned exactly the rows of the page that was left (30 of 30 and 24 of 24 matched, none invented). So the empty answers are not a model that stopped answering.

**What this does not show, and why it matters here.** These are clean synthetic pages where a field is cleanly missing. The hallucinated values in the real test split (`docs/eval/hallucinations.md`) are a different situation: blurred phone photos, a vendor line the model reads as "Tokopedia" or as a shop name, a date whose year digits are "slightly blurry; 2028 is what is legible" (confidence 0.7), and, above all, three photos that may be scored against the wrong label. Nothing in this probe resembles a page where the text is present but unreadable; it measures whether the model invents what is plainly not there, and it does not. The result therefore narrows the question, it does not close it: the open risk is the model filling in what is smudged, cropped or glared over.

**The follow-up this points to** (not done, not in scope here): a smudge probe instead of an erase probe: the same documents with the value blurred or lowered in contrast until it is no longer readable, which is the condition the real misses have; and real-document probes with boxes measured in the label app. If the rate stays near zero there too, the real-test cases are label and policy problems; if it does not, they are model failures and the abstention score (which did not see them: 9 of 27 were at confidence 0.9 or more) is the next thing to fix.
